The cost of a gap in the lead tape

Every splice failure stops the insertion line

Axial and radial insertion machines consume component lead tape continuously. When the tape runs out or a splice fails, the machine waits and the board stops.

Insertion line downtime

An empty lead tape stops the machine mid-cycle — every minute lost multiplies across the shift.

Missed insertions

A weak splice lets the lead tape gap open, so components miss their insertion station.

Wrong hole format

Three-hole and five-hole machines are not interchangeable; the wrong tape jams the feeder.

Heat at the joint

A splice that softens near the soldering side can release during the run.

Adhesive lifting

Low-tack tape lifts under tension and lets the splice pull apart mid-feeding.

Procurement fragmentation

Different insertion machines and lead pitches need different tape — sourcing it per machine is slow.

How to splice lead tape

The splice that keeps axial and radial leads feeding

AI splice tape joins the tail of one component lead tape to the head of the next, so the insertion head never runs empty.

01Prepare the tail of the running lead tape and the head of the new tape
02Align the three-hole or five-hole pattern and overlap the ends
03Apply the splice tape and press for full adhesion
04The insertion machine continues — no gap, no missed component

Technical evaluation FAQ

Questions buyers ask before ordering

What is AI splice tape used for?

AI splice tape joins the tail of one component lead tape to the head of the next on axial and radial auto insertion machines. It keeps the lead tape feeding continuously through a change, so the insertion head never runs empty.

What is the difference between three-hole and five-hole tape?

The hole pattern must match the insertion machine's lead-tape drive. Three-hole tape suits one family of machines and five-hole tape suits another. Send the machine model or a sample tape and we confirm the correct format.

What material and adhesive does it use?

The base is crepe paper with a polyurethane high-tack adhesive rated at 300 N/m. Thickness is 0.15–0.5 mm depending on the type, giving a strong, thin splice that passes the feeder.

Can it withstand heat near the soldering process?

Yes. The heat-rated type withstands 150 °C short-term and 100 °C long-term exposure, keeping the splice stable near the soldering side of the line.

Is there a reference drawing for radial lead splice tape?

Yes. The reference is the SPLICING TAPE RADIAL LEAD drawing, Universal Instruments C24782000, material 3M 2364, kiss-cut at 2.75-inch intervals with 60 yards per roll.

How is it packed and how fast can you ship?

Rolls are packed per machine family. Standard formats ship from stock; non-standard widths, hole patterns and heat ratings are confirmed per order with a sample-first process.
