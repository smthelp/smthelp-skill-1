The print defect hiding in your cleaning cycle

Poor wiper paper quietly taxes every board you print

Under-stencil cleaning is a key process step in solder paste printing. The wrong paper leaves paste in apertures, smears the stencil underside and drops transfer efficiency where it hurts most — fine pitch.

Apertures staying plugged

Residual paste in small apertures cuts transfer efficiency — at an area ratio of 0.50 you can lose half the deposit, causing opens on 0402s and fine-pitch CSPs.

Underside smearing

Paste smeared on the stencil bottom side transfers to incoming boards and becomes solder balls and bridging between fine-pitch leads.

Lint left behind

Cheap wipes shed fibers into apertures during the dry stroke; the next board prints through a partially blocked opening.

Paper that weakens when wet

Cellulose-heavy sheets swell and tear mid-stroke under vacuum, stopping the printer and wasting the cleaning cycle.

Wrong core, jammed roll

Each printer brand uses a different take-up shaft — cross notch, one-word notch, 12-rib groove or paper tube. A mismatched core slips or jams.

Procurement fragmentation

Buying a different wipe per printer brand multiplies SKUs, suppliers and qualification work for one consumable.

Wet · Vacuum · Dry

What the paper must survive inside your printer

Modern screen printers clean with a programmed sequence of strokes. The wiper roll must wet evenly, hold its strength under vacuum and leave zero lint on the dry pass.

02Vacuum stroke — particles pulled out of apertures into the sheet
03Dry stroke — fresh dry paper sweeps the stencil underside clean
04Apertures clear — transfer efficiency restored for the next board

Technical evaluation FAQ

Questions buyers ask before ordering

What is the paper made of and why 55/45?

The sheet is 55% native wood pulp (cellulose) for absorbency and 45% polyester for strength, bonded as a spunlace double layer at 56 gsm / 0.3 mm. Cellulose grabs flux and solvent fast (≤0.5 s absorption); polyester keeps the sheet from swelling, weakening or linting during wet and vacuum strokes.

Which screen printers does it fit?

Reference specifications cover DEK, MPM, GKG, EKRA, JUKI, Fuji, Yamaha, Panasonic/Matsushita, KME, Hitachi, Sony, Minami and Desen cleaning units. If your printer is not listed, send the six parameters — core length, core ID, notch, roll OD, material width, material length — and we make it.

How do I choose the right size and core?

Tell us the printer model, or measure your current roll: core inner diameter, core length, paper width and paper length, then the notch type (cross “+”, one-word “−”, 12-rib groove, smooth PVC or paper tube) and winding direction. The fit guide above lists the most common brand specifications.

Does it work with IPA and water-based cleaning agents?

Yes. The roll supports wet, vacuum and dry cleaning strokes with standard production cleaning agents including IPA and water-soluble chemistry. The sheet is engineered to wet out on a controlled area without changing its mechanical properties.

Is it really lint-free?

The spunlace structure leaves enough distance between fibers to trap solder particles without releasing fibers, and wear testing over sharp aperture edges shows no lint deposited on the stencil. That is what keeps apertures clear for fine-pitch printing.

What is the packing and how fast can you ship?

Rolls are individually wrapped and packed 20 per carton, sized to the roll format. Standard specifications ship quickly from stock; custom specifications are confirmed within 24 hours with a sample-first process and about a one-month customize solution window.
