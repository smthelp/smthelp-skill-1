The defect hiding in your conveyor

When the board shakes, the wave solder defects appear

When you see bridging, skips or icicles, the first suspects are flux, solder and temperature. Often the true culprit is mechanical — inconsistent PCB stability during transport.

Bridging

Vibration and sagging change the board's contact with the wave, and excess solder bridges fine leads — the classic mechanical-caused defect.

Solder skips

An unstable board lifts out of the wave at the wrong moment; leads miss the solder entirely and rework catches it later.

Icicles

Inconsistent wave contact pulls solder into points as the board exits — another signature of unstable transport, not bad flux.

Board warp and sag

Heavier, larger and thinner PCBs flex during preheat and soldering; standard claws can't hold the board flat.

Wrong claw geometry

An "almost right" claw with the wrong size or angle still fails: parts near the board edge collide, or the board slips.

OEM part bottlenecks

Original fingers are slow and expensive to replace — every day a conveyor waits is a day of lost output.

The thermal journey

The claw is the only component that touches the PCB the whole way through

From flux application to the exit of the cooling zone, the conveyor claw maintains contact, angle and stability. Its performance is fundamental to the soldering process.

Technical evaluation FAQ

Questions buyers ask before ordering

Why titanium, and why does material quality matter?

Titanium withstands the heat of the wave without degrading, resists corrosion from flux and solder chemistry, and holds its dimensions so the board angle stays constant. A weak or plated claw can deform, wet with solder, or shed particles into the process — all defect sources.

What is the difference between L-type and V-type fingers?

L-type gives maximum edge support for standard PCBs where component clearance is not an issue. V-type is designed for boards with components close to the edge, providing clearance without sacrificing a secure grip.

How do I identify the right claw for my machine?

Start with the machine brand and model, then send clear photos of the worn claw from several angles (ideally next to a ruler). For full certainty, send a physical sample — we replicate it exactly, including bend angles and hole positions.

What about heavy, large or thin boards that sag?

Standard claws may not prevent warping on heavier boards. Our dedicated heavy double claw (multi-part JT10434-series) is rated up to 80 kg of support and configurable, holding boards flat through preheat and soldering.

Do you supply claws for Vitronics Soltec, ERSA and Kurtz machines?

Yes. Reference fittings include the Vitronics Soltec finger L-shape C450126-B and C450001 family (Dover Soltec L 423210 drawing) and ERSA/Kurtz finger types supplied per quotation. Send your part number or drawing for confirmation.

What is the lead time for standard and custom claws?

Stocked models (200+) dispatch quickly from inventory. Custom claws start with the 3-step match process — confirm the specification within 24 hours, then manufacture from sample or drawing. Exact delivery is confirmed per order.
